What the rules say about writing to people you sell to in the EU and in Romania, where to read them, and what Superstar does about each.

## The short version

- **Researching** business contacts can rest on legitimate interest under GDPR. **Emailing** them is a separate question, decided by the ePrivacy rules of the country you write to.
- **In Romania, Germany and Poland**, a commercial email needs the person's prior consent, company addresses included. Reach those accounts through a warm intro, an inbound lead or your own customers. France allows B2B email when it's about the person's job.
- **Everyone** may object to direct marketing at any time, and you have to tell them so from the first message.
- **LinkedIn and X** have their own automation rules, linked below. Superstar's agents work through your profiles in a browser: they like, follow and invite, and a person validates every post, reply and comment before the Publisher sends it. How far to go is your team's call.

## GDPR: storing and researching contacts

Recital 47 says direct marketing "may be regarded as carried out for a legitimate interest", taking into account what the person can reasonably expect ([gdpr-info.eu](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-47/)).

The EDPB's Guidelines 1/2024 on legitimate interest (version 2.0, adopted 10 November 2026) set three steps, all of which must hold: a legitimate interest, necessity, and a balancing test. Paragraph 111 says legitimate interest is not available "if the direct marketing at issue is unlawful" ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-11/edpb_guidelines_202401_legitimateinterest_v2_en.pdf)). Legitimate interest can cover keeping a researched contact in **People**. It doesn't make an email lawful where the email rules require consent.

Write down a short legitimate interest assessment for your Companies and People tables, and keep a **Source** on every row. The Sales desk tables have the column.

## ePrivacy: email and messages

Paragraph 113 of the same guidelines: under the ePrivacy Directive and national laws, unsolicited direct marketing by email, SMS, "messenger and other kinds of similar applications" needs **prior consent**. Once consent is required, the processing "may not be based on Article 6(1)(f)", the legitimate interest basis ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-11/edpb_guidelines_202401_legitimateinterest_v2_en.pdf)). The Court of Justice held in case C-102/20 that the list of channels is not exhaustive.

We don't know how far "similar applications" reaches. It may cover LinkedIn messages and WhatsApp, not only email. Treat a LinkedIn message as if it might be covered: personal, with a reason, one at a time.

Member states may treat company subscribers differently from individuals, so B2B email rules vary by country. Check the rules of the country you write to. We don't publish a table per country without a legal source for each.

## Romania: Law 506/2004, article 12

What the article says, from a consolidated copy ([legeaz.net](https://legeaz.net/legea-506-2004-prelucrare-date-caracter-personal/articolul-12)). Check it against the official text on [legislatie.just.ro](https://legislatie.just.ro/):

1. Commercial communications by email are forbidden unless the subscriber gave **prior express consent**.
2. **Soft opt-in:** you may email your own customers, whose address you got during a sale, about similar products, if they could object for free and simply when you collected the address and can in every message.
3. Never hide who is sending, and always give a valid address to stop the messages.
4. Paragraphs 1 and 3 apply to subscribers that are **legal persons** too. A company address doesn't exempt you.

ANSPDCP, the Romanian data protection authority, has fined senders for messages without consent, for example Elefant Online, 10,000 lei ([Financial Intelligence](https://financialintelligence.ro/operatorul-elefant-online-a-fost-amendat-cu-10-000-lei-pentru-ca-a-transmis-mesaje-comerciale-nesolicitate/)).

We are not sure whether one individual email, written to one person about their situation, counts as a "commercial communication" under article 12. The law has no exemption for one-to-one email, and the fines we found were all for messages to consumers. Until counsel says otherwise, reach Romanian accounts through:

- a [warm intro](/superstar/playbooks/warm-intros/), where the connector asks first;
- an [inbound lead](/superstar/playbooks/inbound-leads/) who wrote to you;
- your own customers, under the soft opt-in;
- a person-to-person conversation on [LinkedIn](/superstar/playbooks/social-selling/), comment first.

## Germany, Poland and France

- **Germany:** UWG §7 treats advertising by email without the recipient's prior express consent as an unreasonable nuisance, for businesses as for consumers ([gesetze-im-internet.de](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html)).
- **Poland:** article 398 of the Electronic Communications Law (in force since 10 November 2024) requires prior consent for direct marketing by email, company addresses included.
- **France:** the CNIL allows email prospecting to professionals without prior consent when the message relates to the person's job, as long as they are told and can object ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-prospection-commerciale-par-courrier-electronique)).

The agents' outreach email skill follows this: for a prospect in Romania, Germany or Poland with no consent on record, they don't draft a cold email.

## The right to object

GDPR article 21 lets anyone object to direct marketing "at any time", and the processing must stop. The right must be "explicitly brought to the attention" of the person at the latest at the first communication ([gdpr-info.eu](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-21-gdpr/)). The EDPB's paragraph 121: this objection is unconditional, there is no balancing.

## Deliverability

Google asks every sender to keep the spam rate below 0.3%, and senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to offer one-click unsubscribe on marketing mail ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). Superstar sends from your own mailbox, one email at a time, so you are far below those volumes. Keep the opt-out line anyway.

## LinkedIn

LinkedIn's User Agreement, section 8.2 (in force from 3 November 2025), forbids using bots or other unauthorised automated methods to send messages, to create, comment on, like, share or re-share posts, or to add connections. It also forbids scraping through software, plugins or browser add-ons ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)).

LinkedIn's March 2026 feed update says it works against comment automation, engagement pods and "comment to agree" bait ([LinkedIn](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)).
