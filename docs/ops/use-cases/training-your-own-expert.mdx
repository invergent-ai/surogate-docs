# Training your own expert

You've been running an agent against a frontier model for a few weeks. The bill is real, latency is OK but not great, and you're paying for general intelligence to do a specialized job. This is the case where Surogate's training pipeline pays for itself: fine-tune a smaller model on the work your agents actually do, then serve the small model cheap.

This is the closing loop of every other use case in this manual.

## What you need

- **A few hundred to a few thousand captured sessions.** Quality beats quantity — 500 clean sessions outperform 5000 mixed ones.
- **A base model to fine-tune.** Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen 3 8B, or similar. Strong enough to learn your task; small enough to serve from one GPU.
- **GPU compute.** One A100 / H100 for a few hours is the typical bill. You connect the cloud backend once; everything else runs through it.

## Set it up

### Step 1 — Build a dataset from your sessions

Train → Datasets → **New Dataset** → **From Conversations**. Pick the agent whose sessions you want to learn from, set a date range, set a quality filter (only flagged-good sessions, or only sessions with no errors, or some other rule).

The dataset lands with kind `sft` — supervised fine-tuning. Surogate Ops formats it as (prompt, completion) pairs and stores it in the Hub.

### Step 2 — Train the run

Train → Training Runs → **New Run** → **SFT**. Pick:

- **Name** — *Support v1*, *PR review v3*, whatever.
- **Base model** — the open-weights model to fine-tune from.
- **Dataset** — the one you just created.
- **Precision** — `bf16` is the safe default; `qlora` is the cost-saving default if you're optimizing for GPU hours.
- **GPU offer** — pick from the offers on your connected backend. An 8B fine-tune on a single A100 80GB is the sweet spot.

Submit. The run lands in the Workload Queue under kind `sft`. Logs, loss curves, and live metrics show up on the run detail.

A couple of hours later it's done. The weights land in the Hub.

### Step 3 — Serve the result

Build → Models → **Deploy Model**, source **Local Hub**, pick the repo from your training run. Surogate spins up a serving container. Wait for **Serving**.

### Step 4 — Redeploy the agent on top of it

Open your agent. Settings → **Model** → swap to the new expert. Redeploy.

That's the loop. Your agent now runs on a model that was trained on the work your agent was doing.

## Verify before you cut over

Don't trust the loss curve alone. Run an **Arena** ([Train → Arenas](../features/arenas.mdx)) with two contestants — your old setup (frontier model) and your new setup (expert) — over a benchmark of representative tasks. Let the Coordinator decide. If the expert wins or ties, cut over. If it loses, look at the failure cases and add them to the next training run.

## When this is worth doing

- You're spending more than $1k/month on inference for one agent. The training run pays for itself in weeks.
- Latency matters and your frontier model's P95 is too slow.
- You have a privacy story that requires keeping inference on your own compute.
- You want a stable model. Frontier models get deprecated; your fine-tuned 8B doesn't.

## When it isn't worth doing

- You have fewer than a few hundred captured sessions. Garbage in, garbage out.
- Your agent's job is genuinely open-ended — wide-coverage chat, anything-goes research. Specialization helps narrow tasks; it hurts broad ones.
