# Templates catalog

A template is a pre-configured agent — a model, a system prompt, a starter set of skills, and (in most cases) a starter knowledge base shape — that you can deploy with two clicks instead of building from scratch. Pick one, name your version of it, and you have a working agent in under a minute.

This page is the catalog. Thirty templates organised into eight categories, each one a job your team can hand off to an agent on day one.

## How templates work

The path from template to running agent:

1. **Find** — Work dashboard → *Describe what you need* → **Find Templates**. The semantic search ranks the catalog against your description. You can also browse the catalog directly.
2. **Preview** — click a template card. You see the default name, the description, the skills it ships with, the default model, and the knowledge base shape (if any).
3. **Deploy** — **Use this template**. The agent is created, configured, and deployed. About thirty seconds for the first deploy of a fresh model; seconds on subsequent deploys.
4. **Customise** — open the agent's **Settings**. Swap the model, attach your own knowledge base, replace any placeholder skills with the real ones. This is where templates pay off: the structure is correct from the start; you just fill in your team's specifics.

Every template's underlying model is configurable. The defaults skew toward general-purpose chat models that handle the category's typical traffic well; pick a different model if you have specific cost or capability needs.

## Sales (7)

Templates that handle the lead-to-revenue surface. All seven assume an outbound or inbound chat channel and integration with a CRM through a skill you wire up.

### Lead Qualification

Triages inbound contact-form submissions: enriches the company through a data provider, scores against your ICP, drops the qualified ones into your CRM with a suggested rep. Out of the box: a `lookup_company` placeholder skill, a `create_opportunity` placeholder, and a single-document ICP knowledge base you fill in with your team's qualification criteria.

### Sales Assistant

A general-purpose chat surface for an account executive — surfaces relevant deals, answers questions about the product, drafts follow-up emails. Pairs well with CRM read skills and your product's documentation as a knowledge base.

### Renewals

Watches the renewal pipeline, flags accounts at risk based on usage signals, drafts personalised outreach. Ships with a `get_account_health` placeholder and a renewal playbook knowledge base shape.

### Account Research

Pulled before an account-executive call. Given a company name, the agent gathers context — recent news, headcount changes, technology stack hints, mentions in your product's logs. Outputs a one-page brief. The template ships with placeholder skills for the data sources you'll wire it to.

### Real Estate Concierge

A property-search assistant for real-estate teams. Translates natural-language buyer requests into MLS-style queries, surfaces matching listings, schedules viewings. Placeholder skills for MLS / Zillow / your CRM.

### Ecommerce Advisor

Product-recommendation chat for an online store. Given a shopper's request, it queries your catalog, narrows by attributes, and presents three options with a justification each. Pairs with your store's product knowledge base.

### Travel Planning

Itinerary-builder for a travel agency or a corporate travel desk. Takes a brief (dates, budget, preferences), produces a draft itinerary with bookable options. Placeholder skills for flight / hotel / ground APIs.

## Support (6)

Templates for first-line response and escalation in support orgs.

### Customer Support

The starter for most teams. Answers product questions from your help-centre knowledge base, escalates cleanly when it can't, writes a ticket through a `create_ticket` placeholder. The default deploy target in [Tutorial: Build a support bot](../tutorials/build-a-support-bot.md).

### Knowledge Base

A pure RAG assistant — answers from your docs and only your docs. No tools, no ticket creation. Useful for internal Q&A surfaces where escalation isn't the goal.

### IT Helpdesk

First-line IT support: password resets, software install requests, status of in-flight tickets. Placeholder skills for your ticketing system; a knowledge base of your IT runbooks.

### Patient Access

A healthcare-domain template for patient-facing scheduling, intake, and triage. Designed for HIPAA-mode operation; the template includes guardrails on PHI handling and an explicit escalation-to-human path.

### Field Technician

In-the-field reference assistant for service technicians: equipment manuals, troubleshooting trees, parts lookups. Optimised for short questions and source-anchored answers.

### Partner Support

Channel-partner-facing support — handles questions from resellers and integrators against a curated subset of your internal docs.

## Success (3)

Templates for the customer-success surface: onboarding, training, education.

### Onboarding

Welcomes new users, walks them through first-run setup, answers setup questions, hands off to a human only when it can't. Pairs with your onboarding documentation as a KB and a skill to schedule a follow-up call.

### Training Coach

A persistent training assistant for a workforce — quizzes, scenario walkthroughs, knowledge-check loops. Backs onto a curriculum knowledge base and a `record_progress` placeholder skill for your LMS.

### Education Tutor

A subject-matter tutor for an educational product. Adaptive difficulty, encouraging tone, citation discipline (every answer points at the source material). Pairs with the course's content as a KB.

## HR (2)

Templates for human-resources-facing chat.

### HR Policy

Answers employee questions about benefits, leave policy, expense rules — anything in your employee handbook. Single-KB template; no external tool calls by default.

### Recruiting Coordinator

Candidate-facing chat for a recruiting funnel. Schedules interviews against your calendar, answers FAQs about the role and the company, captures candidate info into your ATS. Placeholder skills for the calendar and the ATS.

## IT (2)

Internal IT-facing templates beyond the Support → IT Helpdesk template.

### Incident Triage

Reads an inbound incident description, classifies severity, suggests a runbook, and creates the incident ticket with the right tags. Placeholder skills for your monitoring and incident-management systems.

### Data Request

Handles "can I get access to / a report on" requests from across the company. Reads the request, looks up the user's role in your IAM, decides whether to auto-approve, escalates if not. Placeholder skills for IAM and the analytics platform.

## Productivity (3)

Templates that compress recurring information-gathering work.

### Meeting Prep

Given a calendar event, the agent assembles a prep brief: attendees, their roles, recent context (relevant CRM notes, recent ticket activity, recent emails). Output is a paragraph or two you read on the way into the meeting.

### Executive Briefing

A daily or weekly digest for an executive: top metrics, anomalies, top deals, top tickets, calendar for the day. Built around scheduled invocation through the API rather than chat.

### Bug Intake

Engineering-facing chat for bug reports. Asks the right questions to produce a high-quality ticket — repro steps, expected vs actual, environment — then files the ticket in your tracker.

## Finance (3)

Templates for finance-team workflows.

### Procurement

Vendor-spend lookup, PO status, approval routing. Reads from your procurement system and writes routing decisions back.

### Finance Ops

Month-end close assistant: cash-flow reconciliation walks, expense categorisation, anomaly flagging. Pairs with your accounting system through skills.

### Claims Intake

Insurance-claim intake chat. Walks the claimant through the required information, files the claim, schedules adjuster follow-up. Designed for forms-heavy domains; placeholder skills for the claims platform.

## Operations (4)

Operations-team templates for dispatch, compliance, and intake.

### Operations Dispatcher

Real-time dispatch for a field operation — drivers, technicians, couriers. Reads inbound requests, matches against available resources, updates the dispatch board. Placeholder skills for your fleet-management platform.

### Compliance Intake

Captures compliance-relevant reports (whistleblower, ethics, conflict-of-interest) with the right confidentiality controls, files them into your case-management system. Designed for sensitive intake patterns.

### Legal Intake

First-line legal-team chat: triages requests, routes to the right counsel, captures the necessary context. Pairs with your matter-management system.

### Product Feedback

Reads inbound product feedback (from chat, support, social, in-app) and classifies it: bug vs request vs general sentiment, severity, the affected feature area. Writes structured records into your feedback aggregation system.

## When templates aren't the right starting point

Three situations where you should skip templates and build from scratch:

- **The shape of your agent doesn't match any template.** None of the thirty fits if your agent's primary surface is, say, a Slack-only audit assistant for a specific compliance regime. Start from a blank agent and wire it directly.
- **You'll throw away most of the template.** If you'd replace the model, the system prompt, all the skills, and the knowledge base, you didn't gain anything from picking a template. Just build it.
- **Your customisation will conflict with the template's defaults.** Templates set system-prompt language that's hard to override completely. If your tone or your behavioural constraints fight the template, start clean.

For the other 95% of cases, picking the closest template and customising is the fastest path to a working agent.
